Payments

How to pay me

I'm based in Damascus, Syria. Most international payment apps don't serve Syria yet, so clients pay me in one of two simple ways. Each takes about 10 minutes, and I'll walk you through it the first time.

Invoice firstYou always get an invoice in my name before you pay.
In US dollarsYou can pay from your own currency; the invoice is in USD.
Small fixesPaid on delivery, after you've seen it working.
Bigger projectsPaid in stages: part up front, the rest on delivery.

Two ways to pay

Fastest · arrives in minutes

USDC, a digital dollar

USDC is a digital version of the US dollar. One USDC is always worth one dollar, so there's no price risk.

  1. I send you a payment link with the invoice.
  2. If you don't have USDC yet, buy it on an exchange you trust, such as Binance, Coinbase or Revolut.
  3. Open the link and send the amount in USDC on the Solana network to the address it shows.
  4. Send me the transaction ID. I confirm as soon as it arrives.

Only USDC, only on Solana. Other coins or networks can be lost. The link shows exactly what to send, so always use the link instead of typing the address.

No crypto needed · cash pickup

Western Union

Send from Western Union's website or app, or at any agent. In the Gulf, many exchange houses are Western Union agents.

  1. I send you my full legal name and city with the invoice.
  2. Choose Syria as the destination and cash pickup as the way to receive.
  3. Write my name exactly as I sent it. It must match my ID letter for letter.
  4. Send me the tracking number (MTCN). I collect it and confirm.

The full invoice amount should arrive. Western Union shows its sending fee before you pay; please add it on your side.

What about a bank transfer?

Not yet. Syrian banks are only now reconnecting to international transfers. As soon as I can reliably receive a normal bank transfer from the Gulf, it will be listed here.

Is this safe for me?

You only pay against an invoice in my name, and both methods give you a receipt with a reference number. For bigger projects you pay in stages, so you never pay much before you've seen the work.

I've never bought USDC. Is it complicated?

The first time takes a little setup, because the exchange verifies your ID. After that it's a few taps. If you'd rather not, Western Union works just as well.

Can I pay in AED or SAR?

Yes. You pay from your own currency: Western Union and the exchanges convert it. The invoice amount is in US dollars.

Why not PayPal, Wise or Payoneer?

They don't serve people living in Syria yet, even after the sanctions ended. The two methods above are the ones that reliably reach me today.
